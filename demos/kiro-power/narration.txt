[b1 — hook]
A spec-driven IDE turns a sentence into working code in seconds. But it
only knows the repository in front of it.

[b2 — connect]
Here, an engineer adds OutcomeOps to Kiro as a single MCP server. One
endpoint, one bearer token, running inside your AWS account. With that,
the IDE can reach the organization's entire knowledge base.

[b3 — spec]
The engineer describes the outcome: build a Lambda handler that processes
a payment refund. Kiro drafts it in seconds. Clean, readable, and missing
the context only the organization has.

[b4 — govern]
On its own, the draft would log the full card number and store the amount
as a float. Then the Power goes to work. It queries the knowledge base and
pulls the standards that govern this code. Each one a real document,
retrieved with a citation and a relevance score. A decision record turns
the amount into a Decimal. A security standard masks the card number in the
logs. An audit standard records who issued the refund, and when. And the
team's handler standard enforces their own validation and response pattern.
Same spec. Now grounded in how the company actually builds software.

it's written. Every time. And these aren't house style. They map to the
standards your auditors actually check: security and risk management, data
privacy and governance, even federal risk and authorization.
